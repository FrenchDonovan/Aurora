      01

      THE RETRIEVAL PIPELINE

      From question to a trace.

      A question is tokenized and ranked against a curated excerpt collection using BM25. Selected passages form a cited, extractive brief. Excluding a paper removes it before retrieval and synthesis run again.

      1. QuestionTokenize the terms.
      2. RetrieveRank available excerpts.
      3. ExtractPreserve source wording.
      4. InterveneRemove a paper. Rerun.
      THE CRITICAL DISTINCTION

      Relevance
      ≠ truth.

      A lexical score is not truth confidence, entailment, citation verification, or a scientific verdict. The collection uses short excerpts; full-paper qualifications may be absent.

      02

      THE LEARNED POLICY

      A small world.
      Real reinforcement learning.

      The RL arena learns which excerpt to select next—or when to stop—under a reading budget. A tabular Q-learning agent explores the environment and updates state–action values from transition rewards.

      TEMPORAL-DIFFERENCE UPDATEQ(s,a) ← Q(s,a) + α [r + γ max Q(s′,a′) − Q(s,a)]

      The state is the selected-excerpt bitmask. Actions select an unread excerpt or STOP. Every run starts from zero Q-values. Seeded training, decision traces, baseline comparisons and Q-tables are exportable.

      WHAT THE REWARD MEASURES

      Coverage.
      Cost.
      Overlap.

      The explicit lexical reward is a proxy. Training and evaluation share one question and environment. This is tabular RL, not deep RL, RLHF, or evidence of generalization.

      02 / THE RL ARENA

      LEARN.
      SELECT. STOP.

      Give the agent a question.
      Make every excerpt cost something.
      Watch a policy emerge from experience.

      THE EXPERIMENTTabular Q-learning selects evidence under a budget. The declared reward measures lexical coverage, reading cost and overlap. Training and evaluation use the same environment; research quality and generalization are not measured.
      01 / DEFINE THE ENVIRONMENT

      Higher cost rewards a smaller evidence set—or stopping immediately.

      Exclude sources before training

      Checked papers are removed from the action space. A new environment gets a fresh policy.

      The reward, in plain sight

      Utility = 3 × coverage − reading cost × selections − 0.25 × overlap.

      Coverage is the fraction of unique question terms present in selected titles or excerpts. Overlap sums pairwise shared question terms, divided by question length. Tags, curator notes and claim labels are unused.

      Each action earns the change in utility. STOP earns zero and ends the episode. The evidence budget ends it automatically.

          What makes this reinforcement learning?

          The state is the selected-excerpt bitmask. Actions select an unread excerpt or STOP. An epsilon-greedy agent explores, receives transition rewards, and learns state–action values using the temporal-difference update Q(s,a) ← Q(s,a) + α[r + γ max Q(s′,a′) − Q(s,a)]. Terminal transitions have no future value.

          Alpha is 0.2, gamma is 1, and epsilon decreases from 1 to 0.05. Every run starts with zero Q-values. The finite, absorbing environment has at most 12 active excerpts. Finite training is not guaranteed to find the optimum. This is tabular RL, not deep RL, RLHF, or a trained language model.

          The exact oracle enumerates feasible subsets. It is a check on the proxy objective, not an independent evaluation of research quality. Query-local learning makes no claim of generalization to new questions. Algorithm reference: Watkins & Dayan, Q-learning (1992) ↗
