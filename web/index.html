      02 / WHAT A SCORE MEANS

      Relevance is a narrow signal.

      A retrieval score estimates lexical relevance to your question. It is not a probability, truth confidence, citation verification, or a scientific verdict.

      Only unchanged, curated development examples can display sample claim relations. Current examples were AI-curated and await independent human adjudication. An edited or new question receives no automatic support or contradiction label.

      The collection uses short excerpts, often from abstracts. Full-paper qualifications may be absent. Inspect the original paper before relying on a finding.

      03 / RESEARCH EVALUATION

      Not measured yet.

      No held-out study has been completed.

      04 / THE NEXT RESEARCH GATE

      Earn the learned component.

      A CPU-trained relevance ranker is planned. It requires reviewed question–passage judgments, question-family-disjoint splits, frozen features, Python/browser parity, and comparison with the same BM25 candidate pool.

      The site checks for an optional model artifact. A missing, synthetic, invalid, or incompatible artifact leaves learned ranking visibly unavailable while the baseline remains usable. A valid reviewed-data pilot must be selected explicitly. Software checks do not establish model quality.

      05 / REPRODUCE A RUN

      Take the receipt.

      Every investigation can be exported as JSON or readable Markdown. The receipt records the question, corpus fingerprint, retrieval method, active excerpts, source exclusions, and limitations.

      Exports contain the active result. An excluded paper cannot remain in its evidence brief. Questions stay in memory until you explicitly download them.

      REINFORCEMENT LEARNING / EVIDENCE UNDER A BUDGET

      Teach an agent when to stop.

      Which excerpt should an agent read next? Train a Q-learning policy, change the reading cost, and inspect the decisions it learns. Every episode runs here in your browser.

      A transparent RL sandbox. This agent optimizes a declared lexical reward for one question. It does not learn scientific truth, verify citations, or replace the retrieval baseline. Training and evaluation use the same environment.
      01 / DEFINE THE ENVIRONMENT

      Higher cost rewards a smaller evidence set—or stopping immediately.

      Exclude sources before training

      Checked papers are removed from the action space. A new environment gets a fresh policy.

      The reward, in plain sight

      Utility = 3 × coverage − reading cost × selections − 0.25 × overlap.

      Coverage is the fraction of unique question terms present in selected titles or excerpts. Overlap sums pairwise shared question terms, divided by question length. Tags, curator notes and claim labels are unused.

      Each action earns the change in utility. STOP earns zero and ends the episode. The evidence budget ends it automatically.

          REPRODUCE THE EXPERIMENT

          Export the reward, settings, source snapshot, learning curve, policy and Q-table.

          What makes this reinforcement learning?

          The state is the selected-excerpt bitmask. Actions select an unread excerpt or STOP. An epsilon-greedy agent explores, receives transition rewards, and learns state–action values using the temporal-difference update Q(s,a) ← Q(s,a) + α[r + γ max Q(s′,a′) − Q(s,a)]. Terminal transitions have no future value.

          Alpha is 0.2, gamma is 1, and epsilon decreases from 1 to 0.05. Every run starts with zero Q-values. The finite, absorbing environment has at most 12 active excerpts. Finite training is not guaranteed to find the optimum. This is tabular RL, not deep RL, RLHF, or a trained language model.

          The exact oracle enumerates feasible subsets. It is a check on the proxy objective, not an independent evaluation of research quality. Query-local learning makes no claim of generalization to new questions. Algorithm reference: Watkins & Dayan, Q-learning (1992) ↗
